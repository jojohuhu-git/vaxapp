# PediVax UX Review — 2026-07-03

Follow-up to [ux-code-review-2026-07-02.md](ux-code-review-2026-07-02.md). That review's roadmap is 7/9 complete;
this pass walks every user path again, addresses the owner's new findings, and designs the two big
consolidations requested (reference-tab merge, forecast layout).

**Fixed in this pass** (branch `forecast-ux-defect-fixes`, stacked on `tab-consolidation-reset-safety-net`):

1. **Today's Visit dated in the past.** With DOB 2/7/26 on 7/3/26, the header read "Jun 9, 2026".
   Cause: age floors to whole months (4mo), and the header printed the *slot's nominal date*
   (DOB + 4 × 30.4375 days) instead of the actual date. Now the Today's Visit header, the current
   table row, the mobile card, and the PDF rows all print the real current date. Any patient whose
   age fell between slot boundaries — i.e. almost every patient — saw this bug.
2. **"N past visits — click to show" did nothing.** The toggle revealed past rows, but the
   progressive-disclosure filter (`showFull`) immediately re-hid them, so clicking only flipped the
   button label. The owner suspected the toggle was pointless and considered removing it; it was
   broken, not pointless. Fixed in both the desktop table and mobile card views.
   **Recommendation: keep it** — seeing "Dose 1 done" rows at past visits is how a clinician
   confirms the history was entered correctly.
3. **Icons + font drift in "Why" explanations.** The Today panel rationale used a 💊 pill emoji
   before brand tips and 🔗 before references, at 13px; the forecast cell popover used 12px with hex
   colors; the optimal-schedule popover used 11px. All three now share 12px, design tokens, and
   icon-free "Label ↗" reference links.

## 1. Per-path confusion audit

Walked with: empty patient → DOB 2/7/26 (4mo, no history) → each tab, desktop + 375px mobile.

### 1a. First load / empty state
- **Still no input on the empty screen** ("Select Patient Age to Begin" is text-only,
  [MainPanel.jsx:85](../src/components/MainPanel.jsx)). This was the #1 finding of the prior review
  and is the single highest-value remaining fix: a busy MA's first 10 seconds are spent hunting for
  where to type. Put a DOB field + age typeahead directly in the empty state (dispatching the same
  actions as the drawer), or at minimum a large "Enter patient" button that opens the drawer.

### 1b. Patient drawer (data entry)
- **OCR drop zone still sits above manual visit entry.** Most users type; the screenshot import is
  the exception. Collapse the drop zone to a slim "Import from image…" button/row below Add Visit
  (prior review §2B, still open).
- **Stale copy:** the empty history table says "Use **Quick Add** above" but the panel it points to
  is labeled **"Add Visit"** (QuickAdd.jsx is an orphaned component). One-line fix.
- **Two parallel entry models remain**: Add Visit (visit-centric) and "+ Show 18 more vaccines"
  (vaccine-centric grid). Prior review suggested demoting the grid to an "Advanced" disclosure;
  still worth doing — two ways to do the same thing means the user has to decide which is "right."
- **Age/DOB conflict still nukes the workspace.** The conflict screen replaces all tabs until
  resolved. Better: resolve inline in the drawer (radio: "Use DOB (4 mo) / Use typed age (6 mo)")
  and never let the main panel go blank.

### 1c. Immunization Schedule tab
- **Today's Visit panel is strong** — status badge, dose N of M, brand picker, combo strip, Why.
  This is the panel a clinician actually needs in the first 2 minutes. (See §3 for making it the
  undisputed center of the app.)
- **Routine table still needs horizontal scrolling** on anything narrower than ~1300px (18 columns).
  See §3 for the redesign.
- **Legend on mobile says "Click a cell for clinical notes"** but the mobile card view has no
  clickable cells (and no Why access at all). Either wire card chips to the same popover or hide
  that legend line on mobile.
- **"Fewest Injections" prints raw ISO dates** ("Visit 1 — 2026-07-03", "2030-02-07 series
  complete") while every other surface prints "Jul 3, 2026". Run those through `fmtD()`.
- **RSV-mAb silently vanishes in Fewest Injections.** The optimizer intentionally skips RSV-mAb
  (`buildOptimalSchedule.js:48` — it's a monoclonal antibody, not a series), but the Today panel
  directly above lists it as due. Add one footnote line: "RSV-mAb is seasonal passive immunization
  and is excluded from series optimization — administer per Today's Visit."
- **Mid-series combo brand switching** in the optimizer output (Vaxelis ×2 → Pentacel at D3 in the
  walkthrough patient) is clinically permissible but surprising; clinicians strongly prefer brand
  continuity. Worth a "why did the brand change?" note or a preference for continuity when
  injection counts tie.

### 1d. Compare Regimens
- Regimen cards are clear. The **Brand Constraints Analyzer below them is a second, manual
  checkbox workflow** whose top output duplicates what the brand dropdowns already enforce. §2
  redesigns this whole tab.
- The analyzer requires re-selecting vaccines the app already knows are due today — busywork.
  Default the checkboxes to all of today's due vaccines (user still can uncheck).

### 1e. Brand Rules tab / Catch-up modal
- Content is good but **both are unscoped reference dumps** relative to the patient on screen
  (catch-up modal always shows all 18 rows). §2 merges them into the patient context.
- "Catch-up Schedule ↗" looks like an external link (↗ elsewhere in the app means "opens a new
  site"); it actually opens a modal. If it stays, use a different affordance ("Catch-up Schedule…").
- CatchUpTab's footer says brand constraints are in "**Plan → Brand Constraints**" — a tab name
  that no longer exists (renamed to Brand Rules). Stale copy.

### 1f. Compliance Audit
- Empty-history state is a plain sentence — fine. With history it's detailed and well-referenced.
  Minor: it's the *first* tab in the bar but is useless until history exists; consider ordering
  tabs by workflow (Schedule first) or auto-selecting Immunization Schedule when history is empty
  (default tab is already `forecast`, so only the bar *order* misleads).

### 1g. Visual consistency (carried forward)
- Vaccine-name colors (18 distinct hues) + status-badge colors = two competing color systems
  (prior review §2C). Recommendation stands: reserve color for *status*, render vaccine names in
  ink with weight for hierarchy.

## 2. Design: merge Brand Rules + Catch-up into the Compare Regimens analyzer

Requirement: one home, no lost context, no redundant display when the three areas share data.

### What each surface uniquely contributes today

| Surface | Unique content | Shared/duplicated content |
|---|---|---|
| Brand Constraints Analyzer (Compare Regimens) | Patient-specific severity-ranked warnings; co-administration notes | Brand age limits, combo dose gates — restated as hardcoded prose in `comboAnalyzer.js` |
| Brand Rules tab | Same-day-safety box; MenB family lock; RV mixing rule; combo dose-gate cards (from `COMBO_DOSE_GATES`); brand age-window notes (`BRAND_AGE_NOTES`) | Everything except the same-day box overlaps the analyzer's subject matter |
| Catch-up Schedule modal | CDC Table 2 min ages/intervals (`MIN_INT`), all 18 vaccines | Interval data also powers dose projections everywhere |

The redundancy the owner sensed is real, and it's structural: `comboAnalyzer.js` hardcodes prose
versions of rules that `brandRules.js`/`BRAND_AGE_NOTES`/`MIN_INT` already encode as data. Two
sources will drift (several analyzer strings cite FDA package-insert framing that the data files
have since superseded).

### Proposed structure — one tab, three progressive layers

Rename **Compare Regimens → "Regimens & Rules"** (or keep the name). Remove the Brand Rules tab and
the Catch-up Schedule tab-bar button. The tab becomes:

```
[ Regimen cards (unchanged) ]

[ Constraint analysis — auto-run, patient-scoped ]        ← replaces checkbox analyzer
   For each vaccine due today (pre-selected; chips can be toggled):
   ● severity-ranked constraint rows (err/warn/info), each row expandable:
       ▸ the combo dose-gate card for any combo it references   (from COMBO_DOSE_GATES)
       ▸ the brand age-window note for that vaccine             (from BRAND_AGE_NOTES)
       ▸ the catch-up row for that vaccine: min age + intervals (from MIN_INT)
   ● co-administration notes (incl. same-day-safety box, shown once at top)

[ Full reference ▾ ] — collapsed accordion at the bottom
   ▸ All combo dose gates (the current Brand Rules cards)
   ▸ All brand age windows
   ▸ Full CDC Table 2 catch-up grid (the current modal content)
```

Rules that preserve "no lost context, no redundancy":

1. **Patient-scoped layer shows only what applies now**; the collapsed "Full reference" holds
   everything, so nothing is lost — it's one click away instead of a separate tab/modal.
2. **Each fact renders once per view.** If a constraint row already displays the Vaxelis dose-gate
   chip inline, the Full reference section is collapsed by default, so the duplication is never
   on screen simultaneously.
3. **Single source of truth**: rebuild `analyzeCombo()` to *generate* its rows from
   `COMBO_DOSE_GATES` + `BRAND_AGE_NOTES` + `MIN_INT` + a small table of co-admin rules, instead of
   parallel hardcoded strings. That converts the drift risk into data — and any future rule edit
   automatically appears in analyzer, reference, and dropdown-filtering behavior consistently.
   (Keep the handful of genuinely prose-only rules — HBIG at birth, febrile-seizure counseling — as
   a co-admin data table.)
4. **Auto-run**: no "Analyze Selected (N)" button. The app knows today's due list; run on render
   with all due vaccines selected, keep the chips so a user can narrow to "what if I only give
   these two today?" That turns a 4-click workflow into 0 clicks for the common case.
5. **Catch-up context lands where it's used**: the catch-up intervals for a vaccine appear inside
   that vaccine's constraint row ("HepB catch-up: D2 ≥4w after D1; D3 ≥8w after D2 and ≥16w after
   D1, min age 24w"), which is what a clinician actually needs mid-visit — not an 18-row grid.

Migration also deletes: `CatchUpTab` modal plumbing in MainPanel, `BrandScheduleTab` stays as-is
(it's a strategy explainer, different job — but consider linking it from the regimen cards since
"Optimal Regimen: Vaxelis" and the Pediarix/Vaxelis/Pentacel comparison belong together).

## 3. Design: Immunization Schedule / forecast layout

Problems: (a) Routine table = 18 columns → constant left-right scrolling, unusable on mobile
(mobile cards exist but are read-only); (b) Routine and Fewest Injections have unrelated layouts
(matrix vs. card list), so switching views feels like switching apps.

### Recommendation: make the *visit card list* the primary layout for both views

The Fewest Injections card list ("Visit N — date, injections, doses") is already the right mental
model — clinicians think in visits, not in vaccine × visit matrices. Invert the current design:

```
Today's Visit panel  (unchanged — it's the workhorse)

View toggle: Routine | Fewest Injections   (same card layout for both)

▸ 3 past visits                                   ← collapsed
● TODAY — Jul 3, 2026 (4 mo)     [highlighted]    ← same content as Today panel, or elided
○ 6-month visit — ~Sep 7, 2026
     DTaP D3/5 · IPV D3/4 · Hib D3/4 · PCV D3/4 · HepB D3/3 · Flu
     [brand pickers inline, same BrandSelect]
○ 12-month visit — ~Feb 7, 2027
     MMR D1 · VAR D1 · HepA D1/2 · Hib D4/4 · PCV D4/4
...
[Full antigen grid ▸]                              ← the current 18-column matrix,
                                                     collapsed behind one click
```

- **One layout, two data sources**: Routine cards come from `buildVisitTimeline` + `dosePlan`
  (exactly what the matrix renders today); Fewest Injections cards come from
  `buildOptimalSchedule`. Same card component → views become comparable at a glance ("Routine: 5
  injections at 6mo / Fewest: 3").
- **The matrix stays** as the collapsed "Full antigen grid" for the users who want the
  column-per-antigen audit view (it's also the only place the per-cell popovers and
  "earliest" move affordance live today — port "earliest" and Why into the card rows, which the
  mobile card items already half-do).
- **Mobile gets the same cards for free** — the current `fcm-cards` duplicate rendering path can be
  deleted rather than maintained in parallel (today it's a second implementation of the same rows,
  minus interactivity).
- **Columns stop being the unit of hiding.** "1 past window (RV) · 3 not yet eligible (…)" chips
  currently manage column overflow; in a card layout, not-due vaccines simply don't occupy space,
  and a single line inside the relevant visit card ("RV window closed at 8 mo") carries the same
  information where it matters.

This is the largest remaining piece of work (est. the size of the mobile-card project), but it
resolves the horizontal scrolling, the two-layout inconsistency, and the mobile read-only gap in
one move, and it *reduces* code (one renderer instead of matrix + mobile cards + opt cards).

Smaller interim fixes if the redesign waits: format optimizer dates with `fmtD()`; add the RSV-mAb
footnote; unify the two views' header/stat strip so the toggle doesn't restyle the page.

## 4. OCR import — making it consistently accurate (recommendations only)

Current pipeline: Tesseract.js `eng`, single worker, 2× canvas upscale when width <1200px, then
line-regex parsing of the raw text stream (`ocrParser.js`). Accuracy varies with screenshot quality
and EHR layout because both OCR *and* parsing assume clean single-column "Name … dates" lines.
Ranked by expected value:

1. **Parse CVX codes when present.** IIS printouts almost always include CVX codes; they're
   numeric, OCR-robust, and unambiguous (`08` = HepB peds, `115` = Tdap, `116` = RV RotaTeq…).
   A CVX→vk (and CVX→brand, since many CVX encode product) table would beat all fuzzy name
   matching where codes exist. This is the single biggest accuracy win and is pure data work.
2. **Use Tesseract's word geometry instead of raw text.** `data.words`/`data.tsv` carry bounding
   boxes. Cluster x-positions to detect the date column(s) and the name column; that survives
   wrapped lines, multi-column IIS grids, and the current failure mode where a date drifts onto
   its own line and the row is dropped as "no antigen match."
3. **Preprocess beyond upscaling**: grayscale → contrast-stretch → binarize (Otsu) on the canvas
   before `recognize()`. Phone photos (shadows, gradients) are the inconsistent case the owner is
   seeing; binarization is the standard fix and is ~30 lines of canvas code.
4. **Surface per-row OCR confidence** in the Review modal (Tesseract reports it per word). Rows
   below a threshold get an amber "verify" outline — focuses the user's manual-review minute on
   the 2 doubtful rows instead of all 12.
5. **Tesseract knobs**: set `tessedit_pageseg_mode` to `PSM.SPARSE_TEXT` or `SINGLE_COLUMN` per
   attempt and take the parse with more recognized rows; whitelist `A-Za-z0-9/():.,- ` to stop
   `l→1`/`O→0` swaps in dates.
6. **Two-digit years and month-name dates** (`10/12/21`, `Oct 12, 2021`) appear in some EHR prints;
   `DATE_RE` currently requires 4-digit numeric years.
7. Longer term: an optional "paste text" path (the deferred IIS-report Item 6 in project memory)
   sidesteps OCR entirely for EHRs that allow copy — same `parseOcrText` downstream, zero new
   parsing code.

## 5. Brainstorm: the 3-minute clinician workflow

Frame: an MA/nurse has the chart open elsewhere, opens PediVax, and needs "what do I draw up
today?" in under 3 minutes. Everything below is ranked by how much it shortens that path.

1. **Empty state = input** (§1a). Second 0–10: type DOB, done.
2. **Auto-run the constraint analysis** (§2). Second 30: due list + warnings visible with zero
   extra clicks.
3. **A single "give these today" summary line** at the top of Today's Visit: "6 injections + 1
   oral · 3 can combine into Vaxelis → 3 injections + 1 oral". The combo strip already exists;
   the sentence quantifying the benefit is what sells it in a hurry.
4. **Print/PDF is the deliverable** — the Shot List PDF is the thing handed to the nurse drawing
   up. Consider moving "Print Visit Summary / Shot List PDF" to a fixed position (they scroll away
   with the header today) and including selected brands + injection sites checklist.
5. **Risk factors as a quick strip, not a drawer trip**: the 2–3 most consequential flags
   (prematurity, asplenia/complement, immunocompromise, pregnancy in adolescents) could be toggle
   chips on the summary bar. Today they're two clicks deep and easy to skip — and they change the
   whole plan (the PCV/MenACWY/MenB high-risk paths).
6. **Undo/confirm feedback when marking doses given.** After "Add Visit," the drawer stays open
   with no confirmation beyond the table updating; a toast ("Recorded 3 doses at 7/3/26 — Undo")
   would prevent the double-entry errors the Compliance tab later flags.
7. **"Since last visit" mode**: for a returning patient (state restored from a share URL), a
   one-line diff — "New since 3/1/26: DTaP D2 recorded; next due 9/7/26" — turns a re-orientation
   scan into one sentence. (Share URLs already round-trip full state; this is presentation only.)
8. **Shared-decision explainer** (prior review §2D, still open): "Shared decision" badge needs a
   tooltip ("Not universally recommended — offer after individual risk discussion. ACIP SDM
   category"). One `title=` attribute minimum.
9. **Print the catch-up intervals on the Shot List** for catch-up patients ("D3 due no earlier
   than 8/28/26") so the front desk can book the follow-up before the family leaves.
